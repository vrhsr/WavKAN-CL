We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "WavKAN-CL: An Interpretable and Parameter-Efficient Curriculum Learning Framework for Inter-Patient Arrhythmia Detection" for consideration as an original research article in IEEE Journal of Biomedical and Health Informatics (JBHI).

Despite achieving high accuracy in controlled settings, the clinical adoption of deep learning for ECG arrhythmia classification is severely hindered by three interconnected barriers: the opacity of black-box architectures, poor generalization to unseen patients under strict inter-patient evaluation, and memory footprints unsuitable for battery-powered wearable devices. Our study addresses these challenges simultaneously by introducing WavKAN-CL, a novel framework that integrates Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) with learnable Mexican Hat wavelet basis functions and a minority-first curriculum learning strategy.

The key contributions of this work are as follows:

1. To our knowledge, this is the first wavelet-parameterized KAN evaluated under the strict inter-patient DS1/DS2 evaluation protocol, providing intrinsic structural interpretability without relying on post-hoc attribution methods.
2. Our minority-first curriculum learning strategy stabilizes decision boundaries against severe class imbalance, achieving a mean Ventricular (V) recall of 0.88 ± 0.03 across 20 independent seeds with a near-zero validation-test gap (≤ 0.01).
3. With only 95,189 trainable parameters (0.38 MB), WavKAN-CL achieves a >95% parameter reduction compared to recent baselines such as MAK-Net (6.1M parameters), with a median inference latency of 0.78 ms per beat on standard CPU hardware.
4. Our framework satisfies the stringent E3C clinical evaluation criteria met by only 4.1% of recent ECG classification studies, achieving this compliance without specialized neuromorphic hardware.

We believe this work aligns well with the scope of IEEE Journal of Biomedical and Health Informatics (JBHI), particularly its focus on AI-based methods for clinical decision support, interpretable machine learning in healthcare, and the development of trustworthy AI systems for safety-critical medical applications. The datasets used in this study (MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database, PTB-XL) are publicly available, and our source code and pre-trained weights are available on GitHub (https://github.com/vrhsr/WavKAN-CL).

We confirm that this manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not currently under consideration for publication elsewhere.
